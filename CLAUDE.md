# Dungeons

A browser tool that generates places for adventure and lets you walk through them: a house of
rooms and caves built stone by stone, in a valley you can walk, with maps and exports. TypeScript,
Vite, three.js. It is drawn with three.js's WebGPU renderer, which falls back to WebGL 2 by itself
where a browser has no WebGPU (`?webgl` forces that). So nothing here is written in GLSL or WGSL:
materials and post-processing are node materials built with three's shading language (`three/tsl`),
and the one renderer serves both. The exception is the procedural textures, whose recipes are GLSL
painted once at start by a small WebGL renderer of their own (`textures.ts`). No server; it is published to GitHub Pages from the `gh-pages` branch by a workflow
on every push to the default branch.

## Running and checking

- `npm install`, then `npm run dev` and open the address it prints. `?seed=<text>` picks the place; `?nointro` skips the opening card; `?nobake` skips the light bake. `?cam=x,y,z,yaw,pitch` puts the camera somewhere and leaves it flying there, for pictures; `V` toggles flying while walking about.
- `npm run lab` opens the material lab (`lab/materials.html`) on port 5178: a small stage with a cliff, boulders, ground with a track, trees and grass, made by the game's code and drawn with the game's materials. Saving `siteMaterial.ts`, `vegetation.ts` or `textures.ts` swaps the new materials in where they stand (a small plugin in `vite.config.ts` sees to it); `K` keeps a picture to wipe against the live one. Work on rock, ground and leaf materials there first, then look in a valley. See the README.
- `npm run buildings` opens the building lab (`lab/buildings.html`) on port 5179: a square where any kind of building `buildBuilding` makes can be added, moved, turned, scaled and reshaped, built by the game's own builders. It is published with the tool. Its kinds, their dealing and the ranges of their measures are in `lab/buildingKinds.ts`; its trees, boulders and brushes (ground, plants, rock) in `lab/nature.ts`; `npx tsx tools/buildingLab.ts 300` builds every kind with its measures pushed past their ends. Run that after any change to `src/core/building.ts`, `src/core/brick.ts`, `src/render/buildings.ts` or `src/render/brick.ts`, and try a change to a building there. Its buildings' materials are made by the same function as the game's, `makeBuiltMaterials` in `src/render/builtMaterials.ts`, from surfaces painted from the same seeds (`BUILT_SURFACES`), so a change there shows in both (a building given its own *Worn*, 0 new to 4 derelict, gets materials of its own with their own wear, the `wear` option of `makeSiteMaterial`); its buildings are built by the game's builders and laid in through `placeHouse` as a town's are, so that they weather and are lit within as in the game; a village church's measures are dealt by `dealChapel` for both. What stays the lab's own: its light within, the shed's dealing, and each kind's measures and their ranges (`lab/buildingKinds.ts`): a new style or a new measure must be added there too. A castle's wall is edited there by hand, corner by corner, through its plan (`planOf`, `castleOfPlan` and `soundWall` in `src/core/castle.ts`), which the game never edits. Figures are added there too, and given orders from a palette (`lab/figureCrew.ts`), finding their way over what the lab has built; `node tools/browser/crew.cjs <folder>` checks that they get round, in and up a house, onto rock and onto a bench. Run it after any change to `src/figures/` or to what the lab builds. `npm run buildings:page` makes the lab one page for an artifact (`--fragment --figures-beside` for claude.ai, with the figures as text files beside it).
- `npm run figures` opens the figure lab (`lab/figures.html`) on port 5180: figures steered from behind or sent about by pointing, on a small stage of steps, ramps, ledges, a bench and bedrolls; published with the tool. `node tools/browser/figures.cjs <folder>` drives it and checks the paces, sitting, lying, climbing, landing and clips that carry a figure along, and the horse's gaits, its legs' reach and its way up the ramp and the stair and not up a ledge; run it after any change to `src/figures/` or to how the body moves (`player.ts`). Horses go about the stage too (`src/figures/steed.ts`), steered by the same keys (`Shift` gathering from a trot into a canter and a gallop) or sent by pointing, with a rider or without. `npx tsx tools/figures.ts <folder>` makes the figures' files (`public/figures/`) from Quaternius's free packs (CC0), unpacked in that folder; the packs are not kept here.
- `lab/slope.html`: a downhill run down a slice of the game's world in winter (a run a hundred metres broad found in `w2` by `npx tsx tools/skiRun.ts w2 [folder]`, `src/core/skiRun.ts`, and kept in `public/slopes/w2.json`, `skiSlice.ts`; the snow at a metre on the world's near land, the land and country about it coarser, `lab/slopeLand.ts`; run the tool again after any change to the world's land, `stream.ts` or `skiRun.ts`), or down the groomed slope it began with (*Slope*, `?land=groomed`), wide turns tightening to short ones, a straight line, a tuck, braking and on (`makeDownhillRun` in `src/figures/alpine.ts`), with the skis' tracks in the snow and views from behind, beside and above; or ridden (*Ride*), the pointer against the middle of the picture asking for a tuck (up), standing tall (down) and turns (aside), carved, or skidded out at the ends of the range, any together, the skier posed live (`Rider`, `riderPose`) and going as the slope, its stance and turns let it; with kickers on the ridden slope, the stance at the lip choosing the trick (tucked a ski jumper's flight, between straight air, tall a backflip, turning a spin with it; `airPose`), and a landing short of the turn going limp (`src/figures/ragdoll.ts`, posed through `Poser.poseJoints`) until *Restart run* or Enter; the skier an adventurer (`?body=` another); *Skier* puts a skiing horse there instead, alone or with a rider, on two long skis or four short (`src/figures/horse.ts`; `?skier=horse|mounted`, `?skis=four`); served by either lab's server. `node tools/buildingLabPage.mjs <out.html> --page lab/slope.html --only clips.glb,superhero-male.glb --fragment --figures-beside` makes it one page for an artifact, the slices beside it in `slopes/`.
- `npm run typecheck` and `npm run build` must pass before a commit.
- `npm run check` (`tools/sites.ts`): generates sites and confirms each is connected and completable, and that no building stands on the track or in another. Run it after any change to `src/core`. `SETTLE=town npx tsx tools/sites.ts 40 valley` gives every valley a town where there is room (or `village`, `hamlet`, `manor`). It takes some twenty minutes now that land is made before it is laid out; `npx tsx tools/sites.ts 200 island` (or `valley`, `mountain`) checks one kind alone.
- `npm run range`: regenerates `public/range.html`, the report of what the generator makes.
- `npx tsx tools/land.ts 12`: builds the valley, its cliffs and boulders for twelve seeds without a browser, and reports rock standing in the doorway, numbers that are not numbers, build time and triangles. Run it after any change to the rock or the ground. `SHEET=0.8` before it (or `?sheet=0.8` in the browser) lays the valley's walked ground in cells of 0.8 metres instead of 1.2 (`VALLEY_GROUND` in `siteMesher.ts`).
- `npx tsx tools/island.ts 4`: the same for islands (`?place=island`). Run it after any change to `src/core/island.ts`, `src/render/island.ts`, `coast.ts` or `islandPlanting.ts`.
- `npx tsx tools/town.ts 3`: the same for towns (`?place=town`). Run it after any change to `src/core/town.ts`, `src/core/coastland.ts` or `src/render/town.ts`; `npx tsx tools/sites.ts 48 town` checks six towns' layouts.
- `npx tsx tools/mountain.ts 4`: the same for mountain tops (`?place=mountain`). Run it after any change to `src/core/mountain.ts` or `src/render/mountain.ts`; add `peak plain` for mountains with the plain shape alone (`?terrain=plain`).
- `npx tsx tools/islandRelief.ts <folder> 1 isle0 isle1`: islands as shaded relief, drawn beside found, with the way.
- `npx tsx tools/stream.ts w2`: makes squares of a world's land at a metre (`src/core/stream.ts`) about each of its places, reports how long each took and how many triangles a mesh of it keeps at each distance, and checks that neighbouring squares meet exactly; then splits the squares about the first place into four at half a metre (`refineSquare`) and checks those too. `walk.html?world=<seed>` walks that land, streamed in (`?fine` with the ground near the walker at half a metre); `node tools/browser/walk.cjs <folder> w2 valley1 island1` walks it to places and keeps pictures.
- `npx tsx tools/route.ts w0`: lays each coastal town's way to its nearest valley head (`src/core/route.ts`), grades the shortest and makes the squares at a metre along it with its bed cut in (`src/core/way.ts`), and checks that they still meet exactly and the bed is where it should be. Run it after any change to `route.ts`, `way.ts` or `stream.ts`. `lab/route.html` walks that way (the walk page's program in route mode, `src/worldwalk/routeMode.ts`); `node tools/browser/route.cjs <folder> w0` drives it against a build served by `npx vite preview` (port 4173).
- `npx tsx tools/body.ts w0`: walks the game's own body (`Player`) over a world's land at a metre as the walk and route pages move it (`src/worldwalk/body.ts`: a patch of ground about it, tree trunks): the route end to end, then through a wood and back; reports whether it got through, how near it came to a trunk, and how far under or over the ground it stood. Run it after any change to `player.ts`, `body.ts` or how trees are placed (`dress.ts`). The world is to become the game, in steps: `docs/06-world.md`, *The world as the game*.
- `npx tsx tools/places.ts w0`: builds the valley head at the end of the shortest town's way into the world as the walk and route pages do (`src/render/placePack.ts`, `src/worldwalk/places.ts`), checks that the squares made about it with its hole still meet, that they meet the place's land at the edge of its square and how steep the band between is, and walks the body from the town into the valley to the house. Run it after any change to `placePack.ts`, `withHole` in `stream.ts`, or how a valley head is meshed.
- `npx tsx tools/world.ts 3 <folder>`: builds worlds (`w0`, `w1`, …; or one seed) without a browser, reports how long each stage took, what was found and why the rest was turned down, and draws each as shaded relief coloured by cover with its places, towns and ways. Run it after any change to `src/core/world.ts`. `world.html?world=<seed>` draws a world's ink map; `node tools/browser/worldmap.cjs <folder> <seed>…` saves it.
- `npx tsx tools/terrain.ts 3 peak <folder> 1.5 [top|country]`: shaded relief of each mountain's land, plain beside worn, as PNG files; for judging and tuning the wear (`WEAR`, `COUNTRY_WEAR`, `NEAR_WEAR` in `mountain.ts`) by eye. `WEAR='{"cut":30}'` before it tries other settings without editing.
- `npx tsx tools/houses.ts 30`: builds thirty canal houses and thirty houses of brick, each alone, and walks the game's own body (`Player`) through each without a browser: up the stoop, in at the door, up every flight to the top. Run it after any change to their plans or builders, or to how the body steps.
- `tools/browser/*.cjs`: drive the real program with the player's body (walk every doorway, play to the end, try to leave the valley). See `tools/browser/README.md`. Run `play.cjs` on a few seeds after any change to the mesher, collision or game. Run `houses.cjs` after any change to the plans or the builders of houses of brick and canal houses.

## How the code is laid out

- `src/core`: the world, one country six kilometres by ten (a setting) in which islands, valley heads, mountain tops and coastal towns are found, up to four of each, none planned, its land lifted and worn by its rivers (`liftAndErode` in `terrain.ts`), each house's ground fitted to it, levelled or terraced (`fit.ts`), with its rivers, cover, towns, roads and names (`world.ts`, `names.ts`; the game builds its places on a world's when asked, a coastal town as the harbour town on the world's land, see `docs/06-world.md`), the site document (`site.ts`, now version 2: places may lie one above another, joined by stairs), the generator (`generate.ts`), the shape of the valley (`valley.ts`: its two sides as lines, and how far any point is inside them), the island that a site may be instead of a valley (`island.ts`: its coast, cove and the plain shape of its land, what is placed on it, and the caverns under it that its sea cave leads to), the harbour town that a site may be instead (`town.ts`: the town laid out on a coast found in a country made by noise and worn (`coastland.ts`), and the land made over where the town needs it), the cathedral that is a harbour town's house (`cathedral.ts`: its plan of places, its towers' rooms and stairs round their walls, its crypt and dungeon, and how high it is made against the town), the mountain top that a site may be instead (`mountain.ts`: its rim, ridge, summits, way and climb, and the plain shape of its land; worn by default, and the country round about made the same way; on a world's summit, the world's own land, with the house's ground fitted into it and the rim and ridge read from it), islands found in a sea of them rather than drawn (`archipelago.ts`: the sea of islands, and the search for one that suits the house), valleys found in an upland (`highlands.ts`: the upland with its glens, and the search for a valley head), land made the common way (`terrain.ts`: lattices of heights, Quilez's eroded fBM and ridged noise, wear by water drops and by frost), the plans of buildings (`building.ts`), the brick buildings of the Baltic towns, dealt by kind and by the clay of the country (`brick.ts`: merchants' houses with their gables to the street, broader houses with their eaves to it, warehouses, churches), the canal houses of Amsterdam, every choice of a house (its outline over the eaves, breadth, storeys, depth, ornament, windows, stoop, lean) from a stream of its own so that any may be fixed (`canal.ts`), the plans of the insides of both, as their kinds were planned, with a steep stair of flights one over another and a check that every room can be reached (`interior.ts`), locks, keys and the solver (`mission.ts`). No rendering.
- `src/render`: site to geometry (`siteMesher.ts`, `geom/`; an island's land, sea cliffs, caverns and mainland in `island.ts`, a mountain top's rock, paths, shelter, cairns and the peaks round about in `mountain.ts` (its great hall's shape in `greatHall.ts`, which a town's hall shares), its jetty, cottages, boats, lighthouse and castle in `coast.ts`, a town's land, harbour, square, shacks, storehouses, fields and outlook in `town.ts`, what grows on it in `islandPlanting.ts`, and the sea in `sea.ts`), materials and textures, the light bake (`bake/`), vegetation, water, post-processing. Rock is in `geom/rock.ts` (carving a solid from a function: marching cubes for caves, an edge-keeping carver for rock in the open) and `geom/rockForms.ts` (the functions: beds, joints, facets, boulders, and the wearing-down of cliffs); trees are grown in `planting.ts`. A cathedral's outside is built in `cathedral.ts`: its walls traced in bands of height, windows with tracery and coloured glass, buttresses, flying buttresses, roofs, towers and spires, the west portal; it and its rooms are laid not stone by stone but as plain surfaces with the courses in the picture (`MasonryStyle.flat`, the `ashlar` recipe in `textures.ts`). Buildings that stand free in the valley, and the town's, are made in `buildings.ts` and laid into the place by `placeHouse` (`housePlacing.ts`, which marks their rooms as indoors and the feet of their walls damp), of plain solids and not of masonry, one function to a style (timber-framed, classical, and classical of wood); `lab/buildings.html` shows them; those of brick in `brick.ts`, whose fronts are plane figures (outlines of gables, niches, arched openings) made solid in layers, so that niches are sunk and portals step back in orders. Canal houses are built in `canal.ts` with the same tools, then their fronts leaned out over the street by moving what was laid (`movePoints`). The insides of both are built from their plans in `interior.ts`: walls between rooms found as whatever of a storey no room covers, floors on beams, whitewash, the stair. Every building that can be entered has clear glass (`pane`), seen through both ways. The hills beyond the valley are one sheet laid in `landscape.ts` and drawn with the far-land material in `siteMaterial.ts`; haze, mist and cloud are in `post.ts`. Level ground has its own material too (`makeGroundMaterial`), which draws turf, earth, shingle and the track from what the sheet says at each point.
- `src/app`: the program: main loop, player, game rules, touch, sound. `loader.ts` is the loading screen: `main.ts` reports each stage to it, and between stages it lets the browser paint, so the page never holds still long enough for a "page unresponsive" prompt. `meshSiteSteps` (a generator, in `siteMesher.ts`) is the long build cut into steps, one `yield` per place; `meshSite` drains it for tools. A new long loop in the build should yield too.
- `src/figures`: figures that are steered or sent about (`figure.ts`): each carried by the game's own body (`Player`, driven by its `steer` rather than keys), its model playing idle, walk, run and sprint blended by the body's real speed and kept in step with the ground (the run and the sprint made in code on the figures' skeleton when the clips are loaded, `gait.ts`: the bearing leg solved so its foot stays put, the free leg keyed as runners swing it, the hips thrown between steps; and three clips of skiing the same way, `skiing.ts`, with skis and poles hung on the bones while they play, `gear.ts`; the downhill turns and five jumps that go between them, `alpine.ts`, each pose a handful of numbers that can be blended and the whole body turned (a spin, a flip), chained by `Figure.chain` and `play`, which hold a jump for the end of a turn, and a cliff drop that goes over a real edge and holds its hang while the body falls; both posed with `posing.ts`, which sets each part by how it lies in the figure's frame, not by the bones' own axes, or through joints placed anywhere, `poseJoints`; a body gone limp, its joints held at its bones' lengths and falling, tumbling and sliding on the ground, `ragdoll.ts`, any body's points and links given as a `BodySpec`; and a horse that skis, `horse.ts`, made in code on a skeleton of its own, posed by a handful of numbers with its legs found from its skis up, or from its hooves where they stand on the ground, and a rider's pose for its saddle; how the horse looks, `horseBody.ts`, a bay, its trunk, neck, quarters, shoulders and upper legs one solid of rounded masses blended into one another and carved by marching cubes (`horseSculpt.ts`, each point given the bones of the masses it was made of), its head and lower legs lofted, with a mane and tail of hair, and its tack when ridden; clothes for any body on the figures' skeleton, `outfit.ts`, a ranger's, each garment the body's own skin lifted off it and smoothed, so that it bends as the body does, the adventurers of `BODIES` (`library.ts`), who ski and ride; the same horse on its own hooves, `steed.ts`, standing, walking, trotting, cantering and galloping, each gait by the order and the time its hooves are down, steered or sent, over whatever ground is under it), and states for jumping, landing, sitting, lying down, climbing a ledge, hopping a low one, playing any clip (the root's travel taken out of a clip and given to the body) and doing one at a spot it is sent to; their bodies and the clips fitted to each (`library.ts`), from `public/figures/`; everything solid they go among, as trees of bounds made once in their own frames and placed in the world (`solids.ts`); and their ways (`nav.ts`): a grid of floors in layers, storey over storey, made in tiles where a way is looked for, searched by A* and pulled straight.
- `src/core/worldLand.ts`: a world's land as a place built in it reads it (its tiles of 5 metres near, its lattice of 8 beyond, mirrored past its edges but the southern, where the sea goes on); a valley's, an island's or a mountain top's `terrain.world` says to read it (a mountain top is drawn on the world's summit, its country the world's), and `src/app/worldPlace.ts` makes the world and its tiles in a worker for the game (`?world=w2&n=2`; every place is in a world, `w2` unless another is named, but for `?world=none`), keeping them in the browser for next time (`worldStore.ts`, keyed by the built address of `src/worldmap/placesWorker.ts`, so the game and the world page share it; not in development). The world page's *Build every place* lays out every place in that worker and keeps the layouts too, which the game then builds without laying out again. A place's frame in its world is `siteFrame` (`worldLand.ts`), its footprint `footprintOf` (`src/maps/footprint.ts`), drawn on the world page's ink map and on the game's (`src/app/worldView.ts`, `M` past the large plan). `WORLD=w2` before `tools/land.ts` or `tools/island.ts` builds on that world's places.
- `src/core/stream.ts` and `src/render/streamMesh.ts`: the world's land at a metre, a square of 256 metres at a time, and its mesh true to within a given distance; `src/worldwalk/` the page that streams it in to walk on (`walk.html`), drawn with the game's own materials, sky, air, grass and trees (`dress.ts`), with touch controls of its own (`touch.ts`); near the walker, when asked, squares split into four at half a metre with tussocks, stones and rills (`refineSquare`).
- `src/core/skiRun.ts` and `src/core/skiSlice.ts`: a ski run found in a world (high where a band a hundred metres broad can be skied, away from what is built), and its slice, the snow on the world's land about it at a metre and coarser further out, as kept for the downhill run's page.
- `src/core/route.ts` and `src/core/way.ts`: ways between a world's places, laid as its tracks are, and a way's bed graded and cut into the squares at a metre (`makeSquare`'s `ways`), drawn as a track by the ground's material; walked on the route page (`lab/route.html`, `src/worldwalk/routeMode.ts`), with an ink map of the stretch it crosses (`drawInkWorld`'s `stretch`).
- `src/maps`, `src/export`, `src/gallery`, `src/worldmap`, `examples/viewer`: maps (`inkWorld.ts` the world's), exports, the gallery page, the world page (`world.html`; the world made in a worker), and a viewer for exported files.

## Conventions

- Everything a generated place contains is decided in `src/core` and recorded in the site document; the mesher realises it. Maps and exports read the document.
- Stones and timber are closed solids: no face is left open, top or bottom.
- Random choices use `Rng.fork(label)` so that adding a choice does not reshuffle existing sites.
- Comments say why. Plain prose, no jargon.

## What has been measured, and what has not

Measured again after the move to WebGPU (November 2026, same desktop, in the app's embedded browser,
at 1252 by 737, drawing frames back to back): about 10 milliseconds with a lake in view and about
13 looking down the track; a site builds in 6 to 8 seconds. (Since then valleys have taken a shape of their own and grown. Measured on 16 November 2026: building the valley's land and rock alone, without a browser (`tools/land.ts`), took 9 to 11 seconds at the old size and takes 12.5 to 15 at the new, about half as large again in area. Drawing from the arrival point, timed in a pane that was not shown, cost the same at both sizes within the noise of the measure; there are about a sixth more vegetation cards.) (Islands, measured on 17 November 2026 without a browser in a cloud container with four cores (`tools/island.ts`): an island's land, rock, buildings and planting take 19 to 25 seconds, against a valley's 16 on the same machine; it has some 560,000 to 900,000 triangles of rock and 60,000 to 150,000 cards. No frame has been timed on an island; the person who asked for islands tried them in their own browser, from a build published as a page, and reported that they load and run with good performance; their screen showed an island built in 39 seconds, its light baked in 33, and 144 frames a second, which is the browser's cap, with 1.3 million triangles and 81,000 cards drawn.) (Mountain tops, measured on 17 November 2026 on the desktop: building one without a browser (`tools/mountain.ts`) takes 57 to 75 seconds, about two thirds of it carving rock, with 1.2 to 1.5 million triangles of rock; `DBG_T=1` before the command says how long each stage took. In the embedded browser one built in 41 seconds and its light baked in 36; frames drawn back to back at 1024 by 768 in a pane that was not shown took about 3 milliseconds, which has not been checked in a shown pane.) (A castle's valley in summer, measured on 20 November 2026 on the desktop at 1024 by 768 from the graphics card's timestamps, frames stepped by hand in a hidden pane: 12.5 milliseconds where the player arrives in the forest, 11.5 looking over the fields at the castle from 55 metres up, 14 over the wooded slope under it, 5 to 6 in the village and the fields, with leaves thinned with distance; 16.6, 19.7 and 22.6 drawing every leaf. See `docs/04-look.md`, *Lighter frames*.) The WebGL 2 fallback draws the same
picture but has not been timed. The paragraph below is the earlier WebGL renderer, kept for comparison.

Measured in November 2026 on a desktop with a Radeon RX 6800, in the app's embedded browser: a
site builds in 5 to 6 seconds (it was 2 before the cliffs were carved; about 1.4 seconds of it is
the valley rim, and the water and its stones added the last second) and its light bakes in 15 to 22
seconds. With a lake in view, a frame at 1063 by 650 took about 15 milliseconds, of which some 6 are
the second drawing of the scene that the water mirrors; away from water it took 8 to 10. (At 1298
by 849 the same view with water took 20, of which 8 are the mirror; the haze, the mist, the far
land's material and its eight thousand trees together are about 1.5 of it; looking down the track,
with no water in view, it took 15, with the ground's new material in it.) Those are
times for drawing frames back to back, not a frame rate seen in play, which sat at the browser's
cap (144, later 60). An iPad was reported to run the earlier, lighter scene well on high; it has not
been tried since the cliffs, the trees with branches, the doubled leaf count, the clouds, the
mirroring water, the haze and the far hills went in, and that is the first thing to try. Sound has been measured for level but
still never listened to.

The scene is now about a million triangles in the site, 450,000 to 600,000 in rock, and 280,000 to
340,000 vegetation cards, of which some 200,000 are leaves (thinned to three quarters and to half at the
medium and low levels of detail).
